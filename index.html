IOT · SMART AGRICULTURE

IoT Smart Agriculture

An end-to-end IoT agriculture system that monitors soil moisture, temperature, humidity, and water-tank level in real time, and automatically triggers irrigation when the soil turns dry.

  • Monitors soil moisture, temperature, humidity, and water-tank level with real hardware sensors
  • Automates irrigation through a relay-controlled water pump when moisture is low
  • Alerts on high humidity / low moisture (buzzer) and low water level (Blynk notification)
ESP32Arduino IDEDHT11HC-SR04Soil MoistureBlynkIoT
Implementation & verification +
Problem

Manual irrigation wastes water and gives farmers no real-time visibility into field conditions such as soil moisture or water-tank levels.

What was built

ESP32 firmware written in the Arduino IDE, wired to a DHT11, an HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, a soil moisture sensor, an I2C LCD, a buzzer, and a 5V relay + mini water pump. The Blynk IoT platform provides remote monitoring and alerts.

Verification

Documented in the project report: six build-and-verification phases — sensor readings, LCD output, relay-driven pump switching, and a Blynk low-water notification all functioned as intended (the report notes the ESP32 occasionally needed a reboot during extended testing).

DATA ANALYSIS · PYTHON

Diwali Sales Data Analysis

End-to-end exploratory data analysis on 11,251 Diwali sales transactions to uncover customer behaviour, demographic trends, and regional patterns — from data cleaning to statistical testing.

  • Cleans and engineers the dataset — missing values, duplicates, ordinal age groups, and spend tiers
  • Explores gender, age, state, product category, and zone trends with a suite of visualisations
  • Validates findings statistically — ANOVA and Chi-square hypothesis testing
PythonPandasNumPyMatplotlibSciPyJupyter Notebook
Implementation & results +
Problem

Raw retail transaction data is hard to act on — without analysis, businesses miss customer segments, regional demand, and product trends.

What was built

A Python data pipeline: pandas import and cleaning (missing values, duplicates, irrelevant columns), feature engineering (ordered age groups, purchase-value tiers), correlation / skewness / kurtosis analysis, and grouped visualisations for demographics, states, and product categories.

Key results

ANOVA shows purchase amount differs significantly by occupation (F = 2.477, p = 0.00166); Chi-square confirms a product-category–zone association (χ² = 1634.97, p ≈ 1.45 × 10⁻²⁹⁶). Findings: female customers drive higher sales, the adult age group spends most, and sales concentrate in Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Karnataka — led by clothing, food, and electronics.
